# AI Companion Relationships in 2026: What 1,709 U.S. Adults Told Us

AI companions are increasingly being used for conversation, emotional support, romance, and other forms of digital interaction. But how do people actually feel about forming relationships with AI?

In August 2026, [AI Girlfriend Coach](https://aigirlfriend.coach/) surveyed U.S. adults about their experiences with AI girlfriend and boyfriend apps, their reasons for using them, and where they draw the line between AI companionship and real-world relationships.

This repository summarizes the key findings from that research, including differences by gender, age, relationship status, income, and AI companion experience.

## Key Findings

The survey found substantial differences in how Americans view and use AI companions:

- **24.6% of retained respondents had used an AI companion**, either currently or in the past.
- **Men were more likely than women to say they could develop real feelings for an AI companion.**
- **Current AI companion users were more likely than non-users to say AI romance could count as cheating.**
- **Romance and sex were not the main attraction for most respondents.** Respondents also considered reasons such as having someone to talk to, feeling less alone, and feeling better or practicing social interaction.
- **Attitudes varied considerably by gender, age, relationship status, and previous experience with AI companions.**

The full results and methodology are available in the [AI Companion and Modern Relationships Survey](https://aigirlfriend.coach/ai-relationship-survey/).

## About the Survey

The survey was conducted through SurveyMonkey Audience on August 26 and 27, 2026. A total of 2,150 U.S. adults responded.

During quality screening, 441 responses showing a repeated first-option response pattern were excluded from the analysis. This left a retained sample of **1,709 respondents**.

Participants were asked about their experience with AI girlfriend and boyfriend apps, whether they would consider using one, their reasons for doing so, their likelihood of developing feelings for an AI companion, and whether romantic or sexual interactions with AI could constitute cheating.

The survey also collected demographic information including age, gender, relationship status, household income, and U.S. region.

## How Many People Have Used an AI Companion?

AI companion apps are still far from universal, but they are no longer a fringe technology.

Among the 1,709 retained respondents, **422 people, or 24.6%, said they currently used or had previously used an AI girlfriend or boyfriend app**.

That means roughly one in four respondents had some firsthand experience with an AI companion rather than knowing about the technology only from media coverage or online discussion.

This distinction became particularly important elsewhere in the survey. People who had actually used AI companions sometimes viewed AI relationships differently from respondents who had never used one.

## Do AI Companion Users Consider AI Romance Cheating?

One of the clearest differences in the survey appeared when respondents were grouped by their own experience with AI companions.

Among people who **currently used an AI girlfriend or boyfriend app, 80.6%** said a partner having a romantic or sexual relationship with an AI could count as cheating.

That figure fell to **60.2% among former users** and **44.0% among respondents who had never used an AI companion**.

In other words, the respondents with the most direct experience of AI companion apps were also the most likely to believe interactions with them could cross a relationship boundary.

The finding challenges the assumption that people who use AI companions are necessarily more dismissive of AI relationships as "just chatting with a bot."

## Men Were More Likely to Say They Could Develop Feelings for AI

The possibility of forming a genuine emotional attachment to an AI companion also divided respondents.

Men were considerably more likely than women to say they could develop real feelings for an AI girlfriend or boyfriend.

Among male respondents, **23% said they could develop real feelings for an AI companion**, compared with **12% of female respondents**.

The gap suggests that attitudes toward AI relationships are not simply about whether people are willing to use the technology. Men and women may also differ in how open they are to viewing an AI interaction as an emotional relationship rather than purely as entertainment or conversation.

## Romance and Sex Were Not the Main Motivation for Most Respondents

AI girlfriend and boyfriend apps are often associated with romance and sexual interaction, but the survey suggests those features are not the main attraction for most people who would consider using one.

Among women, just **5.2% said affection, romance, or sexual interaction would be their main reason for using an AI companion**. Among men, the figure was **16.0%**, making men roughly three times as likely to choose this motivation.

For most respondents, the appeal lay elsewhere, including having someone to talk to, feeling less alone, or using an AI companion for emotional support and practice with social interaction.

The results suggest that reducing AI companionship to virtual romance or sex misses much of what potential users say they are actually looking for.

## Loneliness and Emotional Support Matter

The survey also points to a broader reason people are interested in AI companions: not everyone is looking for a simulated romantic relationship.

Respondents could choose motivations such as having someone to talk to, feeling less alone, receiving affection or romantic interaction, and feeling better or practicing social interaction.

This matters because AI companion apps are often discussed primarily as substitutes for girlfriends, boyfriends, or sexual partners. The responses suggest that companionship itself may be just as important to understanding why people are interested in these systems.

For some potential users, the value of an AI companion may be less about replacing a human relationship and more about having an always-available conversation partner.

## What the Findings Suggest About AI Relationships

Taken together, the findings show that AI companionship does not fit neatly into a single category.

People may use the same technology for very different reasons. For one person, an AI companion may simply be someone to talk to. For another, it may provide emotional support, romantic interaction, or a relationship that feels meaningful enough to create boundaries with a human partner.

The cheating results are particularly revealing. Respondents with firsthand experience of AI companions were more likely to recognize romantic or sexual AI interactions as something that could affect a real-world relationship.

At the same time, relatively few respondents identified romance or sex as their primary motivation for potentially using an AI companion.

As AI companions become more personalized and capable of remembering previous conversations, the distinction between using a chatbot and maintaining an ongoing relationship with one may become increasingly difficult to define.

## AI Companion Memory Testing

Survey data tells us how people perceive AI companions, but understanding the technology also requires testing how these apps behave over time.

AI Girlfriend Coach separately tests the long-term conversational memory of popular AI companion platforms. Each app is given a specific personal detail during a conversation. The conversation then continues for more than 50 messages across several days before the companion is tested on whether it can recall that information.

The goal is to measure something that can significantly affect the feeling of continuity in an AI relationship: whether the companion actually remembers details from earlier conversations rather than treating each interaction in isolation.

The ongoing results are documented in the [AI Companion Memory Test](https://aigirlfriend.coach/companion-chatbot-memory-test/).

Memory is particularly relevant to AI relationships because an assistant that remembers previous conversations, preferences, experiences, and personal details can create a very different interaction from a chatbot that effectively starts over each time.
